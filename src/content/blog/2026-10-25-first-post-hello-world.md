I finally have a personal website. 🎉
For my first post, I thought I’d share the backstory.

## So why a website?
Either it’s a mid-life crisis or I needed a new hobby. It’s cheaper than owning a car in Singapore, so I’m calling it a win.
It started when I began writing about tech and posting on LinkedIn.

## Down the rabbit hole
While preparing my next post (teaser: it’s AI-related), I needed to do some research and testing. 

**Problem:** I had no lab. I could run Virtual Machines on my home PC, but let’s just say it’s not ideal.

Then I discovered home labs. Don’t worry, there will be interesting topics coming out of this.
That led to another rabbit hole: a refurbished enterprise mini-PC, bare metal, then VMs and LXC containers. 
And, of course, the inevitable thought: if I’m running a server at home anyway, why not host my website on it too?

## Plot twist: My internet connection had other plans
Before committing to hosting the website at home, I set up [Healthchecks.io](https://healthchecks.io) to monitor my home labs' connectivity. 
Week one: multiple “down” alerts. Ouch. Back to the drawing board.
The last two weeks have been quiet, though. No alerts. Perhaps it knows I’m watching.

## The new plan
I like a challenge, so I took the opportunity to try something new:
- **CI/CD** — Automating the process of building and deploying a website.
- **Static sites** — Understanding how to deliver a fast, simple website without needing a traditional server to generate every page.
- **Serverless architecture** — Learning how cloud services can handle parts of an application without me managing the underlying servers.
- **Full-stack serverless applications** — Eventually going beyond a simple website and experimenting with applications that have both front-end and back-end components.

Quite a lot to learn from what started as “I should get a website.”

## The almost 100% free-tier challenge
Another goal is to build and run as much as possible using free-tier services.
Well, almost 100%. The domain registration doesn’t count. The internet isn’t completely free, unfortunately. 😄
I’m more than happy to pay for premium services when there’s a genuine need. For now, I’d like to see how far the free options can take me while I learn.

---

So, that’s the story behind this website.

What started as a way to share my tech posts has turned into a home lab project, a cloud learning exercise, and probably a few more rabbit holes waiting to happen.

Let’s see where this journey takes me.
